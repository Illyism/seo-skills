## How-to — reverse image search

**Title:** How to Do a Reverse Image Search (Desktop and Mobile)
**Slug:** `reverse-image-search`

A reverse image search is the act of searching the internet with an image rather than keywords.

It helps you uncover more details about the image itself and its subject matter.

Such as:

- The original size of the image
- Alternative sizes
- Other appearances on the internet
- Visually similar images

In this article, we'll cover how to do a reverse image search on desktop and mobile.

And how you can get attribution for any of your uncredited images.

## Definition — what is SEO

**Title:** What Is SEO? Meaning, Examples & How to Optimize Your Site
**Slug:** `what-is-seo`

SEO (search engine optimization) is a set of processes aimed at improving a website's visibility in search engines, like Google, with the goal of getting more organic traffic.

SEO activities can take place both on-site and off-site. That's why you may often see SEO divided into "on-page" and "off-page" categories.

In practice, SEO typically involves:

- Keyword research
- Content creation and optimization
- Technical optimization
- Link building

## Debate intro — frame a fight

From [Max Marchione's BPC-157 article](https://x.com/maxmarchione/article/2061492144636920206). The opening:

> BPC-157 is one of the peptides that's talked and asked about the most.
>
> The debate sits between two extremes.
>
> On one side, people say it's the holy grail of healing. On the other, people say it will give you cancer. The answer lies somewhere in the middle.

Then a question list that doubles as the table of contents. Why it works:

- **It frames a fight, not a topic.** Two extremes, and the article as the level-headed referee. That tension is the reason to keep reading.
- **It names the enemy and the method.** "Everyone on Twitter is shooting from the hip. This piece does the opposite." Then it sells a payoff: you'll understand the topic better than almost anyone arguing about it online.
- **Question-list roadmap.** The questions mirror what a curious reader already wonders, and the H2s answer them in order.
- **One-line paragraphs control pace.** "This is where things get murky." Standalone lines give the eye a rest and add weight.
- **Connecting phrases carry the reader.** "Which leaves an open question," "On top of this," "More precisely." Each section hands off to the next.
- **Honest hedging builds credibility.** "Treat the loud voices on both sides with skepticism, including this one."
- **No em dashes.** Ideas are split into separate sentences instead.
- **Concrete over vague.** "16 patients," "87.5%," named people. Specific numbers make claims feel checked.
